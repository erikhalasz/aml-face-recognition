AML project · VGGFace2 → LFW · 23–24 Nov 2026

ResNet-18 + ArcFace face recognition, trained and tested

We implemented the project deck end to end on a laptop GPU (Quadro T2000, 4 GB). The steps were data preparation, alignment, baselines, three planned training runs, one improved run, and evaluation on LFW. The final model reaches 97.87% LFW verification accuracy, which beats the 97% target. It meets four of the five committed targets.

The identification threshold is chosen on VGGFace2 dev identities, as the deck requires, and gives 0.96% FAR there. It is never tuned on LFW. On LFW the same threshold lets in more unknown people, because LFW impostor pairs score higher. Fitting it on LFW would hit 1%, but that would be test-set leakage.

What was done, in order

Setup

Environment and data

Created a Python venv with PyTorch 2.5.1 (CUDA 12.1), MTCNN, OpenCV and scikit-learn. Unzipped the VGGFace2 subset (483,919 images, 7,382 identities). Downloaded LFW and the official pairs file.

Split + leakage control

Identity-disjoint split

Held out 500 random identities as dev, because the archive has none of the official test identities. Removed 506 identities whose names match LFW people (46,569 images). That leaves 394,027 training images from 6,409 identities and 27,563 dev images from 467 identities.

Preprocessing (≈ 2 h)

Detection → 5 landmarks → 112×112 alignment

Ran MTCNN on every image and applied a similarity warp to the ArcFace template. Quality filters dropped 8,781 blurry, 3,391 low-confidence, 3,260 no-face and 328 badly exposed images. A fixed face mask taken from training images matches LFW to the masked VGGFace2 crops. A crop-only copy was made for Ablation 2.

Baselines

Classical and reference models

Eigenfaces (PCA, 200 components) and LBP histograms set the lower bound. A pretrained FaceNet was run through the same LFW code to check that the evaluation is correct (97.67%).

Diagnosis

Why LFW was lower than expected

LFW faces are about 100 px, blurry and JPEG-compressed, while the training crops are sharp. There was also a small scale and position offset in the aligned frame. Dev accuracy could not show either problem.

24 Nov 09:38 → 20:39

Run 4: ArcFace + degradation augmentation, 14 epochs

Training images were randomly downscaled (30–90%), blurred, JPEG-compressed and colour-jittered, and given small scale, shift and rotation changes. The run used 14 epochs instead of 8. It reached 97.87% on LFW and became the final model.

Report

Notebook, README and leakage scan

notebooks/report.ipynb holds all the tables and figures, including ROC and DET curves, ablations, threshold transfer and failure cases. The README has the commands and the results.

24 Nov ≈ 21:15

Embedding leakage scan

The final model compared every LFW person with every training identity. 21 LFW people turned out to be in the training set under a different spelling of their name, which the name filter missed (for example Sean Combs / Diddy, Dwayne Wade / Dwyane Wade). They appear in only 53 of the 6,000 test pairs. Without those pairs the final model scores 97.85% instead of 97.87%, so the results are not inflated.

Training: dev-set verification accuracy per epoch

6,000 sampled pairs from the 467 held-out VGGFace2 identities, measured after each epoch. All runs: batch 32, SGD with lr 0.035 and cosine decay, fp32, about 47 min per epoch at 144 images/s.

On dev, the final run ends at 95.45%, almost the same as the plain run (95.20%). On LFW the two are 3.4 points apart. Augmentation helps with the gap in image quality between VGGFace2 and LFW, and the dev set has no such gap, so its accuracy can't show the gain.

Ablations

Ablation 1 · loss

ArcFace vs softmax

−0.26 pp

94.52% vs 94.78% on LFW, which is within one fold standard deviation. At 8 epochs the ArcFace margin hasn't paid off yet: the ArcFace loss starts much higher (about 43 vs 9 for softmax), and the literature shows its advantage after 20 or more epochs.

Ablation 2 · preprocessing

5-point alignment vs crop only

+1.57 pp

92.95% → 94.52% on LFW. Rank-1 rises by 4.8 pp (89.7% → 94.6%) and FRR halves. On dev the two were level, because the VGGFace2 crops were already roughly aligned. On LFW's natural photos, alignment matters.

Extra · training recipe

Degradation aug + 14 epochs

+3.35 pp

94.52% → 97.87% on LFW. EER fell from 5.65% to 2.37% and TAR at 0.1% FAR rose from 61% to 91%. The spread across folds also fell, from ±1.33 to ±0.40. This was the biggest single improvement.

Engineering problems solved along the way

Mixed precision was 11× slowerThe T2000 (TU117) has no tensor cores, so fp16 runs at 0.42 TFLOPS against 1.97 in fp32. All training runs in fp32.
NaN loss on the first stepCaused by cudnn.benchmark. Turned it off.
Training at 17 img/spermute() produced channels-last tensors, which are about 10× slower on this GPU. Adding .contiguous() brought it to 144 img/s.
Slowdown at batch 48Windows spills VRAM above about 3 GB into shared memory. Used batch 32 with lr 0.035.
Domain gap from masked training cropsThe VGGFace2 subset comes pre-aligned with a white mask. A fixed mask estimated from training images is applied to train, dev and LFW. The alignment template is zoomed 1.2×.
Non-ASCII paths and Windows workersImages are read with np.fromfile + imdecode. Memmaps are opened lazily so DataLoader spawn workers don't pickle them.
Jobs stopped under memory pressureHappened twice (preprocessing and the softmax run). Every stage is resumable, and the final runs were moved to their own PowerShell window.
Name filter missed alias spellingsThe embedding scan found 21 LFW people under different names in VGGFace2. Re-scoring LFW without those 53 pairs changes accuracy by at most 0.04 pp for every model.
Leakage scan crashA pandas 2.2 groupby.apply change dropped the key column. Replaced it with groupby.head.

Not done, and what to try next

  • FAR ≤ 1% at a dev-chosen threshold. LFW impostor scores sit higher than VGGFace2 dev scores. The fix would be a validation gallery that looks more like LFW, such as degraded dev images, not tuning on LFW.
  • Remove the 21 alias identities from training in any future run. Their effect on the current results is negligible, but a clean split is better practice.
  • ResNet-50 and the CFP-FP / AgeDB stretch goals. These were not attempted. R50 would take about 3× longer per epoch on this GPU, roughly 30+ h for a comparable run.
  • A fair ArcFace vs softmax comparison would need both losses trained with the final recipe (augmentation, 14+ epochs). At 8 epochs the comparison is inconclusive.
